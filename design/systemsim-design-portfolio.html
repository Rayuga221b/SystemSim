01 · Brief

Who this is for, and what it has to feel like

SystemSim is used in short, high-focus study sessions — not casual browsing. The identity has to earn trust fast: technical enough to be credible to senior engineers, warm enough to make failure feel like learning instead of grading.

Audience

Software engineers prepping for system-design interviews. Time-pressured, technically confident, allergic to fluff. They want signal, not decoration.

time-pressuredsenior-leaning

Personality target

The best professor you ever had: clear, structured, never condescending. Rewards curiosity, never punishes mistakes.

clearconfidentwarm

Avoid

Bloated diagramming-tool chrome (too many panels, no soul) on one side, bare LeetCode utilitarianism (zero craft) on the other.

corporatesoulless
02 · Mark

Three directions, one signal

Every concept starts from the same idea: the product's entire interaction model is nodes connected by edges. The mark should be that idea, not illustrate it.

Concept B

Chip Pulse

A component under load — hexagon chip with a live waveform through it. Reads as "monitoring," less as "SystemSim."

Concept A · selected

Pulse Node

Three nodes, one flowing edge, tracing an "S." The center node is the one under simulation — always live, always green.

Concept C

Layer Stack

Literal architecture-diagram shorthand: client / server / data tiers. Clean but generic — reads as "any dev tool."

SystemSim

The center node pulses — a small, quiet cue that something is always simulating. Works from favicon size up to a splash screen.

DiscordCase studyStorage

How Discord's storage engine buckled under a billion daily messages

Problem

A single Cassandra table grew past a trillion rows. Wide partitions turned routine reads into full-partition scans, and tail latency exploded during peak hours.

Solution

Migrated hot data to ScyllaDB with bucketed partitions sized to stay under 1MB, capping partition growth and restoring predictable p99 latency.

07 · Principles

What holds this together

01

The canvas is the hero

Every screen structure — rail, sidebar, panel — exists to serve the graph in the middle. If chrome ever competes with the canvas for attention, cut the chrome.

02

Never color alone

Bottleneck, healthy, and down states always pair color with an icon and a label. Colorblind-safe by default, not by afterthought.

03

Mint is earned, not decorative

The green accent only appears when something is actually healthy or actively simulating. It never shows up as a stylistic flourish.

04

Mono means "measured"

If IBM Plex Mono is on screen, it's a number, a tag, or a piece of state — never a stylistic choice for its own sake.

05

Every case study ends in an action

Reading about a failure is not the product. Simulating it is. The CTA at the bottom of every case study is non-negotiable.
